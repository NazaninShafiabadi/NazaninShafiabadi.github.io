About Me

I am a PhD Researcher in the MLIA team at the ISIR lab (Sorbonne University), working under the supervision of François Yvon. My work bridges Artificial Intelligence and the social sciences. My primary research investigates the expression of bias in multilingual language models—specifically, how these biases vary across languages and how machine translation systems can introduce or amplify cultural distortions in subjective content, such as stance or opinion.

I am currently funded by Bpifrance as a member of the "AI For Democracy Democratic Commons" global research program. In this context, I study the application and impact of AI systems within democratic processes. Ultimately, my goal is to develop language technologies that preserve semantic and pragmatic invariance across languages, ensuring that AI is equitable, transparent, and culturally aware.

Research Interests

Multilingual NLP Evaluating and achieving semantic and pragmatic invariance across languages and translation systems.
AI Fairness & Bias Mitigation Identifying how translation systems introduce or amplify social, cultural, and subjective biases.
AI for Democracy Exploring the safe, equitable usage of AI systems and LLMs within democratic processes.
LM Interpretability Understanding lexical acquisition and the emergence of language abilities in neural language models during pre-training.
